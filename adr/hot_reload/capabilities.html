Domain 7

Capability policy (WASI)

The sandbox in Domain 2 doesn't have to mean the app author loses filesystem, network, or stdio. Both can be true at once, and it's worth being precise about why.

D7.1

WASI is configured permissively by default — filesystem, network, and stdio

reserved

Filesystem via WasiCtxBuilder::preopened_dir("/", DirPerms::all(), FilePerms::all()); network via modern wasmtime-wasi's socket support (.inherit_network() or explicit permissions — this needs the preview2/component-model WASI surface, not base preview1, which has no sockets at all); stdio via the custom sink from D6.4, never inherit_stdio(). Practically the same freedom a native dylib would have had.

Reasoning

Fuel/epoch interruption (D5.1) and linear-memory isolation are properties of wasm's execution model — Cranelift-inserted checks, the sandbox's own memory boundary — and hold regardless of what capabilities the guest is granted. Portability and the ABI fail-safe (D2.2) are equally unaffected. None of the properties that motivated D2.1 depend on capabilities being restricted, so there was no reason to withhold them by default and every reason not to, per D1.3.

Consequences

Marked reserved, not accepted: the shape (WASI wired, configured permissively) is decided, but WASI isn't wired into any draft at all yet — today's guests have zero fd/network/env access because nothing is wired, not because anything is deliberately withheld.

D7.2

Capability sandboxing against a malicious author is explicitly out of scope

accepted

What's given up by D7.1: a genuinely malicious app, once given real filesystem and network access, can do real damage. That's a known and accepted consequence, not an oversight.

Reasoning

Directly follows from the threat model in D1.2 — this was already decided, not a new tradeoff being introduced here. Going permissive on capabilities is just following through on it consistently rather than accidentally reintroducing a sandbox story nobody asked for.
